## Top 5 ranked findings, by expected dollar impact

I rank by *expected impact at this capital, over the next 90 days*, with no claim that these will dominate; lower-severity items may matter more in particular weeks.

1. **F-04 / F-12 — Polymarket-consensus arm and the broader shadow-to-live reconciliation problem.** The arm has 86 settled markets, 260 contracts, 5 day-clusters, +0.12c net. The shadow said +6.39c. The trade history is candid; the finding is structural. The dollar impact of continuing to allocate live slots to a strategy whose realised edge is zero at n=86 is the opportunity cost of the slot — modest in absolute terms, but it is the single most defensible evidence-doctrine finding in the system. *Reasoning for ranking:* the methodology implications propagate to every other shadow, and a system with eight paper arms and multiple shadows will repeat it.

3. **F-05 / F-20 — Lead-lag's +1.86c/contract period-D edge is on283 contracts and is structurally eroded by ~2.1–2.4% settlement basis.** This is the operator's only live arm with a non-zero settled record. The recent fill path is fundamentally sound (the trade history's diagnosis is good), but the headline figure cannot be trusted as the expected per-contract value until the settlement basis rate on the recent week is re-measured. *Reasoning for ranking:* lead-lag is where all the live money is, and the +1.86c headline does not survive a 2% loss-of-notional event. The conservative path is to halve the per-sweep size until the weekly settlement-basis read lands.

4. **F-01 — Polymarket US paper-lab fee model is broken.** Every post-9/17 paper-lab decision (8 paper arms) is biased by ~16% of the fee term. The bias is in the *positive* direction (paper P&L is overstated). This affects the paper labs' verdicts and any "ready" promotion. *Reasoning for ranking:* the labs are the system's pre-live gate for Polymarket US; a +16% fee understatement on the gate biases every downstream decision. Cheap fix.

5. **F-08 — Kill-switch denominates against equity but trips only on settled P&L.** A regime break with60 open positions held at mark-to-market loss will not trip the kill switch until settlements land, by which point the day's loss is at or near the slot cap. *Reasoning for ranking:* this is the largest single open risk-control gap. The 2026-09-13 kill-switch trip happened *because* six coins settled within hours; the same regime break distributed across 24-hour settlement windows would not have tripped.

6. **F-16 — Mean-reversion arm has no measured prior edge for what it actually trades.** The Becker audit was a taker backtest on non-weather markets; the live arm is a maker on weather. The arm generates signals and vetoes them. This is documented as a finding, not a defect — but its presence in the ladder means the system holds the "only backtested edge" narrative for an arm that cannot fire on the population that was backtested. *Reasoning for ranking:* the decision this finding forces ("park mean-reversion or change its seat") is cheap to make and removes a confusion in the operator's record.

(Honourable mention: F-14 multiplicity correction across 16+ arms. Lowest direct dollar impact, highest methodological significance.)

## Strongest disagreement

The thing the operator most believes that I think is most likely wrong:

**The lead-lag edge on period A's terms is reproducible at the current latency.**

The trade history's own analysis isolates *latency* as the cause of the regime break and restoration of latency as the cure. The trade history does not commit to the period-A magnitude being reachable again; it says "the gap between A's +8.94c and D's +1.97c is unexplained on 283 contracts over two days; it needs a week." I read that as honest. But the operating model — `leadLagMaxContractsPerOrder` was *already reduced* from 8 to 1 as part of the cure, the proven-coins band is BTC/ETH/HYPE, the per-window cap is $15 — implies an end-state where the live edge is +1–2c/contract, which is structurally near zero after settlement basis (F-20). The path forward the operator is on is "restore the conditions of period A and watch the edge return." My read is that period A's edge is the lucky tail of a strategy whose base rate is closer to period D's +1.97c, and that "restore the conditions" without restoring the *unlucky* period-A quirks (size 713 contracts of which ~62 were in the basis-mismatched 2% subset, multiplying to ~13 lost contracts averaging −60c = −$8) is what period A actually measured.

The disagreement is: the operator's model has lead-lag as a *latency edge*. I read it as a *dispersion edge whose expected value is dominated by the right tail of the dislocation distribution and the left tail of the settlement-basis rate*, and the live settings (1 contract, $15/window, 2 coins/direction) are pinning the strategy to the left tail. A 5p speed-up from 14s to 70ms does not change the basis rate; it changes the dislocation distribution slightly. The right way to test this is the cadence-shadow read on 2026-09-21, which compares 10s and 60s on the same dislocations, and the lead-lag basis cells read. Until those land, I would not scale lead-lag above 1 contract per sweep regardless of latency.

I commit to this: lead-lag at 1 contract, $15/window, BTC/ETH only, with weekly basis reads, is the right shape for the next month. Everything else is variance.

## Closing

The system is honest. The trade history, the backlog, the developer handbook, the pre-registrations, and the change record read as one continuous attempt to test claims against evidence. The defects are catalogued, the trade history is candid about what worked and what did not, and the methodology bar (where it is applied) is high. The defects are at the seams between the methodology and the practice — the shadow-to-live gap (F-04, F-12, F-36), the fee-model bugs (F-01, F-03, F-10), the settlement-basis blind spot (F-20), the kill-switch denominator (F-08), the multiplicity across many arms (F-14). All of these are tractable, and most of them are already on the operator's list. The report is, more than anything, a confirmation that the project's own evidence doctrine is the right one and an identification of where its current practice lags it.
