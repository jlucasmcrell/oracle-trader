F-01 / methodology / high / verified
Location: scripts/backtests/leadlag_counterfactual.py:91-92
, leadlag_orderbook_baseline.py:101-102, politics_favourite_live.py:37,155, calibration_slopes.py:207-212 vs src/main/ladder/ladder.ts:283,352-359
, src/main/strategies/polyPaper.ts:86

Claim: The research scripts that produce the record's published confidence bands use a clustered SE without the finite-cluster correction and with a fixed normal multiplier, contradicting the correction the app itself adopted after §127 — so every small-cluster band quoted in the docs is too narrow.

Evidence: The scripts compute se = math.sqrt(sum(v*v for v in g.values())) / n
 and return m, m - 1.28 * se, m + 1.28 * se (leadlag_counterfactual.py:91-92). The ladder's own clusteredSe carries the comment "The G/(G-1) finite-cluster correction. Without it this estimator is biased LOW, worst at the three-to-six clusters that a few days of trading produces - which is precisely when the ladder is deciding whether to double a live arm's size on it" (ladder.ts:354-359), and clusterT's comment reads "with two day-clusters the correct multiplier is 1.376, so a band drawn with 0.84 is 39% too narrow" (ladder.ts:283). Even polyPaper applies the correction: Math.sqrt(days/(days-1)*...)
 (polyPaper.ts:86). The research scripts never got either fix.

Failure: The trade history's headline — graded signal "+1.35c/contract (n=354, 80% band [+1.07, +1.63])" — is a G=2 band. Drawn with the ladder's own convention (t at 1 df, plus √(G/(G−1))=1.41 SE inflation) it widens to roughly [+0.4, +2.3]. Same convention produced the era-D gap-bucket cells at n=4–8, the per-coin reads, the politics closure, and every ** significance flag in the calibration-slope tables ("flag = ' **' if (m - 1.28 * se) > 0'", calibration_slopes.py:211). The pending verdicts — gap floor 10-02, ETH/HYPE 10-21, coin-cohort auto-narrow 10-04 — will all be read through these scripts. A band 2–3× too narrow converts "undecided" into "confirmed" for zero-edge rules.

Test: Re-run one existing output (the era-D counterfactual) with clusteredSe's G/(G−1) and clusterT/clusterT95; the gap-floor pre-registration's judgment should require the ladder's helper, byte-identical.

F-03 / fees / medium / verified (structure)
Location: src/main/util/kalshiFee.ts:39-51
 (fee model), docs/FEE-MODEL-VERIFICATION-LIVE-2026-09-18.md
, TRADE-HISTORY:229

Claim: The Kalshi taker fee at mid prices (1.75c) exceeds the entire planned net edge (+1.5c). Any signal with gross edge under ~2c at mid is structurally unprofitable as a Kalshi taker; only maker entries ($0 on plain quadratic series), extreme-price entries (fee → 0 as p→0/1), or venues that pay makers can monetize thin edges. The record knows the fee but never states this as a constraint on its strategy space.

Evidence: Fee = ceil₄ₖₗ 0.07 × C × p(1−p); at p=0.5 that is 7×0.25 = 1.75c > +1.5c. The 6c-floor lead-lag trade at mid gives 29% of its raw signal to the venue before latency costs.

Failure: Arms with 1–2c gross signals get tried as takers and bleed by construction; the fade's maker-first design and mmsim's maker test are the right responses, but the record's "strategies not tried" section doesn't apply the fee-floor filter to its own candidate list.

Test: For each arm, state entry style against its fee floor before any new live test. (One line in the handbook's §12 doctrine would do it.)

F-04 / correctness / medium / verified
Location: src/main/engine/engine.ts:678
, src/main/ladder/ladder.ts:1128,1244

Claim: computeLivePnl caps details at the newest 1,000 settlement rows, and the ladder's evidence gatherers join against that capped list — so evidence silently truncates once a stage outlives 1,000 account-wide settlements.

Evidence: engine.ts:678: details: [...settlements].sort((a, b) => b.timestamp - a.timestamp).slice(0, 1000). leadLagEvidence: pnl.details.filter((d) => d.timestamp >= since && swept.has(d.marketId)) (ladder.ts:1244); quoterEvidence filters the same list (ladder.ts:1128). The account already has 1,420 lifetime settled markets (per the record), and lead-lag alone adds ~30–70 settlements/day at era-D volume.

Failure: A stage whose since predates the 1000th-newest settlement loses rows from every read: n shrinks, the mean re-biases toward the newest settlements, and clusters vanish — with no error surfaced. Latent today for young stages; guaranteed to bite at current volume.

Test: Assert settlement-store count vs details.length
 in getLivePnl (or give the evidence path a since-bounded query). One log line at the boundary makes it observable.

F-05 / fees / low-medium / verified (code) + hypothesis (impact)
Location: src/main/strategies/leadLag.ts:672,707
, src/main/util/kalshiFee.ts:15,96-103,174
, scripts/backtests/leadlag_counterfactual.py:25

Claim: The lead-lag fee gate hardcodes the 1× taker rate while the venue bills sub-1× multipliers on other series; if any KX<COIN>15M series carries a multiplier ≠ 1, both the live gate and the graded signal misprice fees by that factor.

Evidence: kalshiTakerFeeCentsFor(kYesAsk, sweepSizeFor(...)) passes no multiplier (leadLag.ts:672/707); kalshiTakerFeeCentsFor(yesPrice, contracts, multiplier?) defaults to rate 0.07 (kalshiFee.ts:174, 96-103). The header documents the hardcode ("leadLag.ts … hardcoded 0.07", kalshiFee.ts:15) and the D1 audit found 0.5×-shaped residual buckets plus 71 anomalous fills ("venue fee implies count/4") never resolved to series. The counterfactual script hardcodes the same 0.07 (leadlag_counterfactual.py:25).

Failure: Conservative if the multiplier is <1 (gate over-estimates the fee, blocks marginal trades) — but then the graded "+1.35c" is also under-stated and the arm is being judged against a pessimistic counterfactual. Material either way at a 1.5c edge: a 0.5× multiplier is worth +0.87c/contract at mid.

Test: One public, unauthenticated GET per series (/series/KXBTC15M
 … KXHYPE15M) for fee_multiplier; cross-tab the 71 anomalous fills by series. Ten minutes, zero cost.

F-06 / correctness / medium / verified
Location: src/main/strategies/leadLag.ts:448-459

Claim: resolveSlug takes tokenIds[0] as the Up token with no verification against the Gamma outcomes array. A single market with flipped token ordering silently inverts every trade on it.

Evidence: Lines 448-459: tokenIds parsed from clobTokenIds, then const upToken = tokenIds[0] (455), cached via slugCache.set
. No outcomes check anywhere in the resolve path; the up-token assumption flows into the WS subscription and the REST book.

Failure: A flipped market's Poly book is inverted, so every computed gap has the wrong sign — the arm systematically buys the wrong Kalshi side, at real money, and it looks in aggregate like one more slightly-negative market. Undetectable without per-market reconciliation; the cache pins the flip for hours.

Test: Assert outcomes[0] === 'Up' (or resolve by outcome name) at resolve time; log-and-skip on mismatch. This is the cheapest fix in the report: one line plus a test with a mocked reversed Gamma payload.

F-07 / methodology / medium / verified
Location: src/main/strategies/polyPaper.ts:52,91,96,107,120
, src/main/strategies/ibkrLab.ts:14
, handbook §107

Claim: Both paper labs charge ~2c/round-trip phantom friction beyond what live fills pay, then anchor each arm's assessment at zero instead of at the benchmark control arm's measured floor — so any true edge below ~4–7c/trade reads negative and the labs structurally cannot qualify plausible microstructure edges.

Evidence: Taker orders are priced at the ask+1c limit and filled AT that limit: price=o.maker?o.limit:side.ask+.01
 (polyPaper.ts:120, with the limit built at :52), exits at exit=Math.max(0,side.bid-.01) (:107). Live IOC fills pay the book's price, not the limit — the lab over-charges by ~2c/round-trip. Assessment: lower!>0?'Promising; paper only':upper!<0?'Negative; reassess':'Inconclusive' (:91) — anchored at zero, while the benchmark arm is labeled 'Control only' and never used as the null. ibkrLab does the same (fee=.01, slippage=.01
, fills at ask+slippage). The friction is deliberate (:96: "Trading exits are takers (bid minus 1c)") — but deliberate conservatism in the fill price combined with a zero anchor is a bias, and the benchmark arm exists precisely to absorb that bias once. Minor doc mismatch: §107 says settlement controls "buy at the ask"; the code buys ask+1c.

Failure: A true +3c/trade edge reads ≈ −1c and is filed "Negative; reassess." The labs can produce false negatives, never false positives — which defeats their stated purpose (qualifying arms for the operator-hold-protected live venue) and is the exact opposite failure mode from the §127-screen issue.

Test: Make the assessment anchor the benchmark arm's lower bound (or charge fills at the book price and keep the +1c as a displayed slippage reserve). Rerun the current standings — if any arm's label flips, the labs have been suppressing a candidate.

Markouts taken within one day share the day's regime; §12.3 says bands are day-clustered, and the app fixed this class of defect everywhere else after §127.

Failure: Direction is conservative (narrower SE makes the "wholly negative" veto easier to fire → arms held, opportunity cost not cash) — but the fade's hold today may be an artifact of a doctrine-violating band, and the record presents it as a settled verdict.

Test: Day-cluster the markout band (the byDay accumulators already exist in the calibration ledger) and recompute the fade's veto. If it flips, the doctrine fix changes a live decision.

F-10 / strategy (missing) / medium / verified-as-missing
Location: docs/BACKLOG.md:281,288,298,1150
 (mention markets), handbook:509 (yt-dlp toolchain)

Claim: The record names the winners' method for mention/"say" markets — "transcript word frequencies (base rates) plus live tracking" (BACKLOG:281) — but only built the base-rate half, and the base-rate half is measured to lose (favourites in mention series lose 5–15c, BACKLOG:1150; the shadow's base rate is under-confident at middle strikes, :298). The live-tracking half is the actual edge: "a Kalshi mention market settles YES the instant the phrase is said" (BACKLOG:288), and the venue pins to 99c only seconds-to-minutes later. Buying YES ≤95c immediately after a detected utterance — on public YouTube captions via yt-dlp, already the record's own data source (handbook:509) — is a latency edge on a free feed that the winners demonstrably monetize.

Failure mode if untried: the program keeps optimizing a −EV base-rate approach on a market whose payoff is mechanically knowable before the venue prices it. Falsification first, never trade first: over ≥10 broadcasts measure (a) utterance→caption lag, (b) caption→Kalshi 95c-pin lag, (c) ask depth ≤95c at pin time. If the book pins within seconds or depth is zero, it's dead — and the test costs nothing: extend the existing hourly OracleTrader-MentionShadow task with a caption recorder for one week. No authenticated session, no scraping of paywalled feeds.
