## 4. Today's fix — the mention shadow was scoring a price that only exists after the answer is known

**The defect.** `python scripts/mention_shadow.py report` has been printing
`Brier base 0.2680 vs market 0.0000` — a market Brier of *exactly zero* over 34 graded strikes. That is an
artefact of the grader, not a property of the market. `grade()` scored `before[-1]`, the last observation at
or before `close_time`. A Kalshi mention market **settles YES the instant the phrase is said, and Kalshi
sets `close_time` to that moment**, so for an early-YES strike the last pre-close quote is the
post-resolution pin. Every one of the 34 graded rows scored a mid of **0.995 (15 rows), 0.005 (18) or 0.015
(1)**, and 33 of 34 had `brier_mkt` exactly 0.0000. Build-queue item 12's go-live trigger — ">= 100 graded
strikes, Brier better than the market price" — was **unreachable by construction**: nothing beats a Brier
of zero.

**A second, provable defect in the same twenty lines.** The counterfactual NO trade could never fire:

    elif bid is not None and (1 - bid) - (1 - p) >= 0.15 and p <= bid - 0.15:

`(1 - bid) - (1 - p)` is `p - bid`, the **negative** of the NO edge — NO costs `1 - yes_bid` and is worth
`1 - p`, so its edge is `bid - p`. The first clause demands `p - bid >= 0.15` and the second demands
`p - bid <= -0.15`; they are contradictory, so the branch was dead code. That branch is the whole strategy
whenever the base rate sits **under** the price, which on the graded data is 19 of 34 strikes — and it is
why the counterfactual P&L line had never printed at all.

**The fix, smallest diff, all inside `scripts/mention_shadow.py`** (a read-only shadow, no money path,
nothing to restart):

- `scored_observation(rows)` — pure — returns the **first** sight of a strike. The base rate is static over
  the period, so the honest pairing is the earliest quote we logged against the base rate we held then. The
  old pre-close filter went with it: under first-sight scoring it is provably inert (see mutant M3).
- `counterfactual(p, bid, ask, y, gap)` — pure — with the NO edge written as `bid - p`.
- `grade_entry(rows, y, graded_at)` — pure — builds the grades row and now also records `scored_ts` and
  `mid_last`, so the settled pin is kept as data instead of being mistaken for a result.
- `selftest` (10 pure assertions, the convention `polymarket_consensus.py` already uses) and `regrade`
  (rebuilds `grades.jsonl` from the observations and the outcomes already on file, no network calls).

**Mutation-proved, per the standing rule.** Scratch copy, one mutant at a time:
**M1** scored row back to `[-1]` -> 3 failures (`scores the first pre-close observation`, `order of the file
does not matter`, `brier_mkt uses the first-sight mid`). **M2** the old NO clause -> 2 failures
(`NO fires when bid - p >= gap`, `NO loses its stake on YES`). **M3** dropping the pre-close filter -> 10/0,
which is why that filter was deleted rather than tested. Scratch file removed.

**What the shadow says now that it can speak.** `regrade` rewrote all 34 rows (previous file kept as
`grades.jsonl.bak-20260916T111006Z`):

    Brier base 0.2719 vs market 0.1328 (lower is better)
    counterfactual 15c-gap taker trades: 15, net -0.636 per contract-sum, mean -0.0424/contract

The market is roughly **twice as good as our transcript base rate**, and the 15 counterfactual trades the
rule would have taken — all NO — lose **4.2c per contract after fees**. The calibration table says why: the
base rate is badly under-confident in its middle, hitting 1.00 in the 0.2-0.3 (n=5), 0.3-0.4 (n=4) and
0.4-0.5 (n=1) buckets. **This is not a verdict.** Item 12 judges at >= 100 graded, and all 34 rows come from
one event (`KXTRUMPSAY-26SEP14`) first seen on one day, so there is exactly **one cluster** and no interval
is computable. It is now, at least, an honest reading, and its sign points against the shadow's premise
instead of at a fabricated zero. Recorded in backlog 12 and 68 for the 09-25 read.
